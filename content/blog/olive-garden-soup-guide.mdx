Olive Garden's Chicken Gnocchi Soup is one of their most popular menu items — and one of the most frequently requested copycat recipes. It is a thick, creamy, herb-scented white soup with soft potato gnocchi, tender chicken, and just enough spinach to feel like you are making a reasonable choice.

The soup is deceptively simple in structure. Understanding the mechanics of how it achieves its creaminess — and where most home versions go wrong — makes this much easier to replicate well.

---

## The Creaminess Problem

Most home versions of chicken gnocchi soup either end up too thin or too heavy. This comes down to two common errors:

**1. Skipping the roux**
A proper roux — butter and flour cooked together before any liquid is added — is what gives the soup body and prevents the cream from curdling or breaking when it is added to hot liquid. Without a roux, you end up with cream that separates into greasy pools on the surface.

**2. Adding cream too early**
Heavy cream should be added toward the end of cooking, not at the beginning. Adding cream early and then simmering it for 20+ minutes causes it to reduce excessively and sometimes curdle, particularly if the soup is at a rolling boil rather than a gentle simmer.

---

## The Flavor Base

Olive Garden's Chicken Gnocchi Soup has a specific flavor profile that comes from the combination of celery, onion, and garlic as the aromatic base (a classic mirepoix variation), plus a blend of thyme and nutmeg that is subtle but unmistakable once you identify it.

**Thyme** adds an earthy, slightly floral depth that reads as "Italian-American comfort food."

**Nutmeg** — used in very small quantities — is the secret that most copycat recipes omit. A pinch of freshly grated nutmeg adds warmth and complexity to cream-based soups in the same way it does to béchamel sauce. It should be unidentifiable as nutmeg but noticeable if left out.

---

## Gnocchi: Store-Bought vs. Homemade

For a weeknight copycat soup, store-bought shelf-stable potato gnocchi (found in the pasta aisle, not refrigerated) works very well. It absorbs the soup broth during cooking, softening to a pillowy texture that holds up in the bowl without becoming waterlogged.

A few notes on gnocchi in soup:

- **Do not pre-cook gnocchi separately.** Add them directly to the soup and simmer for 8–10 minutes until cooked through. They absorb flavor from the broth this way rather than cooking in plain water.
- **Add gnocchi later in the cooking process.** If gnocchi cook too long in cream-based soups, they can dissolve partially and thicken the broth too aggressively. Add them with 10 minutes remaining.
- **The soup will thicken upon standing.** If reheating leftovers, add 2–3 tablespoons of chicken broth or milk to restore consistency.

---

## The Spinach Question

Olive Garden uses shredded or julienned spinach in this soup — not whole baby spinach leaves, and not chopped spinach. The spinach should be added during the last 2–3 minutes of cooking, not at the beginning.

Spinach added early turns army-green and develops a slightly bitter, overcooked flavor. Spinach added in the last few minutes maintains its bright green color, stays tender rather than mushy, and keeps a mild flavor that complements rather than dominates the cream base.

---

## Chicken: Shredded vs. Diced

Olive Garden's version uses small, shredded pieces of cooked chicken — not large diced cubes. Shredded chicken distributes more evenly through the soup, gets into every spoonful, and creates a more cohesive texture.

**Best approach:** Use a rotisserie chicken and shred the breast meat by hand into small pieces. Alternatively, poach 2 chicken breasts in the chicken broth before building the soup (remove them when cooked, shred, then return them to the pot).

---

## Step-by-Step: The Correct Order

Getting the order of operations right is what separates a good chicken gnocchi soup from a great one:

1. Sauté onion, celery, garlic, and carrots in butter until softened (5–6 minutes).
2. Sprinkle flour over the vegetables and stir constantly for 2 minutes — this is your roux.
3. Slowly pour in chicken broth while whisking to prevent lumps. Add thyme and a pinch of nutmeg.
4. Add shredded chicken. Simmer 10 minutes.
5. Add gnocchi. Simmer 8–10 minutes until cooked through.
6. Reduce heat to low. Pour in heavy cream. Do not boil after this point.
7. Add spinach. Stir and serve within 3 minutes.
