Eggs are the cornerstone of breakfast and professional kitchens alike. Yet, most home cooks consistently overcook scrambled eggs into rubbery curds, struggle to peel boiled eggs, or break yolks when flipping over-easy eggs.

Mastering eggs requires understanding how egg proteins coagulate under heat: **egg whites set at 144°F (62°C), while yolks set at 158°F (70°C).**

---

## 3 Essential Laws of Cooking Eggs

### 1. Low Heat for Scrambled Eggs
High heat forces protein strands to shrink tightly, squeezing out moisture and leaving scrambled eggs watery and dry. Always scramble over medium-low heat with constant gentle folding.

### 2. The Ice Bath Rule for Boiled Eggs
Whether cooking 6-minute soft boiled eggs or 11-minute hard boiled eggs, plunge them into an ice-water bath immediately upon removing from boiling water. This stops heat transfer instantly, prevents green sulfur rings around yolks, and contracts the egg inside the shell for effortless peeling.

### 3. Vinegar Whirlpool for Poaching
Adding 1 tablespoon of plain white vinegar to simmering water lowers pH, causing egg white proteins to firm up quickly around the yolk when dropped into a gently stirred whirlpool.
