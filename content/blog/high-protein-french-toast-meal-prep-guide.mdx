French toast is one of those breakfast foods that feels indulgent even when it is not particularly complicated — pan-dipped bread, pan-fried in butter, dusted with powdered sugar. Standard French toast delivers about 8–10g of protein from the eggs and bread, with most of the calories coming from refined carbohydrates.

The high-protein version of this recipe changes the custard base in two specific ways that dramatically increase protein without altering the fundamental experience of eating French toast: adding blended cottage cheese and a small amount of vanilla protein powder. The result looks, smells, and tastes like French toast. The protein count is 25–30g per two-slice serving.

---

## Why Cottage Cheese in French Toast Custard Works

The classic French toast custard is a mixture of eggs, milk, vanilla, and cinnamon. The eggs provide the binding and protein; the milk provides moisture. Blended cottage cheese replaces or supplements the milk component — and here is why it works without making the toast taste like cottage cheese:

1. **Cottage cheese completely loses its characteristic texture when blended.** A high-speed blender turns full-fat cottage cheese into a smooth, silky liquid indistinguishable from thick cream in texture.

2. **The flavor is mild.** Full-fat cottage cheese has a neutral, faintly milky flavor that is entirely masked by vanilla, cinnamon, and the Maillard browning from the pan.

3. **The protein contribution is significant.** Full-fat cottage cheese provides 25g of protein per cup, compared to 8g in whole milk per cup. Replacing half the milk with blended cottage cheese roughly doubles the protein in the custard.

The resulting French toast is slightly denser and creamier in texture than the standard version — which most people find more satisfying, not less.

---

## Adding Protein Powder: When It Works and When It Doesn't

Protein powder can be incorporated into the custard, but it requires some care. The rules:

**Use vanilla-flavored whey or casein protein powder.** Unflavored protein powder tends to add a slightly eggy or chalky taste at cooking temperatures. Vanilla protein powder integrates invisibly into the custard and adds no detectable flavor difference.

**Use ½ scoop (15–20g) per 4-slice batch.** More than this starts to make the custard gummy and the toast too dense. A half-scoop adds 10–15g of protein to the entire batch (approximately 2.5–4g per slice) without affecting texture.

**Blend it in with the cottage cheese.** Whisking protein powder into cold milk by hand often leaves lumps. Blending everything together ensures smooth, even distribution.

## The Meal Prep Method

French toast holds well for 3–4 days refrigerated, making it an ideal Sunday meal prep. The key is not to overcoat the bread before cooking — slightly under-soaked bread that is cooked slightly under-done will finish cooking when you reheat it and taste far better than fully-soaked bread reheated from cold.

**Step 1: Make the custard**
- 4 large eggs
- ½ cup full-fat cottage cheese (blended smooth)
- ¼ cup whole milk
- ½ scoop vanilla protein powder
- 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
- 1 teaspoon cinnamon
- Pinch of nutmeg
- Pinch of salt

Blend all ingredients until completely smooth.

**Step 2: Choose the right bread**
Thick-cut bread is essential for French toast that holds together and has textural contrast. Brioche provides the richest result. Texas toast (the thick-sliced white bread, usually ¾ inch or more) is a budget-friendly alternative. Whole-grain bread adds fiber but has a denser, less custardy result.

Standard sandwich bread is too thin — it absorbs the custard too quickly, becomes soggy, and falls apart when flipped.

**Step 3: The soak time**
Dip each slice in the custard for **10–15 seconds per side**, not longer. Over-soaking leads to eggy, custard-heavy interior and difficulty getting the outside properly browned. For thicker bread (brioche), 20–25 seconds per side is appropriate.

**Step 4: Cook at medium heat in butter**
Butter over medium heat, not high heat. High heat browns the exterior before the interior custard sets, leaving the center raw. Medium heat for 2–3 minutes per side gives the custard time to set all the way through while the exterior develops a golden-brown color.

**Step 5: Batch cook and cool before storing**
Cook all slices, let them cool completely on a wire rack (not stacked — stacking traps steam and makes them soggy), then layer in an airtight container with parchment paper between layers. Refrigerate for up to 4 days.

## Reheating Without Ruining Texture

**Best method: toaster.** 2–3 minutes in a toaster or toaster oven restores the slightly crisp exterior. This works better than a microwave for preserving texture.

**Acceptable method: microwave + 30-second pan finish.** Microwave for 45–60 seconds to heat through, then place in a dry pan over medium-high heat for 30 seconds per side. This approximates the toaster result.

**Do not microwave alone.** The exterior becomes soft and the interior turns slightly rubbery.

## Toppings That Add Protein (Not Just Sugar)

- **Greek yogurt** (instead of whipped cream): adds 8–10g protein, more tart, less sweet
- **Nut butter drizzle**: almond or peanut butter adds 4–7g protein and slows sugar absorption
- **Cottage cheese and fresh berries**: instead of maple syrup; adds 12g protein, reduces sugar dramatically
