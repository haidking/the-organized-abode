When you need a warm, comforting bowl of soup on a chilly evening, heavy cream soups can leave you feeling weighed down. 

This **Creamy Tuscan White Bean & Spinach Soup** delivers rich, velvety satisfaction in just 20 minutes — using blended cannellini beans instead of heavy cream or flour to achieve its luxurious texture.

---

## Why You'll Love This Recipe

- **Naturally Creamy:** Blending a portion of the white beans creates a thick broth naturally packed with plant protein and soluble fiber.
- **20-Minute Meal:** Uses canned beans and fresh spinach for lightning-fast weeknight preparation.
- **High-Fiber & Heart-Healthy:** Packed with plant-based protein, iron, folate, and antioxidants.

---

## The Secret to Creaminess Without Heavy Cream

Traditional cream soups rely on butter, flour roux, or heavy whipping cream. In this Tuscan recipe:

1. **Simmer Beans & Broth:** Canned cannellini beans are simmered with garlic, onion, and Italian herbs in vegetable stock.
2. **Partial Immersion Blend:** Use an immersion blender to pulse about 1/3 of the pot. The starch from the puréed beans instantly thickens the liquid into a silky golden broth while keeping whole tender beans intact.
3. **Parmesan Finish:** Freshly grated parmesan melts into the warm broth, adding nutty umami depth.

---

## Pro Tips for Maximum Flavor

- **Fresh Spinach at the End:** Stir fresh baby spinach into the hot soup right after turning off the stove. The residual heat wilts the leaves into tender ribbons in 60 seconds without overcooking or turning bitter.
- **Crusty Bread Pairing:** Serve with warm garlic bread or toasted sourdough for dipping into the rich broth.

---

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I make this soup vegan?
Yes! Simply replace the parmesan cheese with 2 tablespoons of nutritional yeast or a plant-based parmesan alternative.

### How do I store leftovers?
Store cooled soup in an airtight container in the fridge for up to 4 days. Reheat gently on the stovetop, adding a splash of broth if the soup thickened overnight.
