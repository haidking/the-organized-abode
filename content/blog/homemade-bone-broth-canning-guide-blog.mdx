Simmering your own beef or chicken bone broth extracts valuable **collagen, gelatin, and joint-supporting minerals**. 

However, storing giant containers of liquid broth quickly consumes all your freezer space. **Pressure canning bone broth** turns your liquid gold into shelf-stable mason jars that last in your pantry for up to **1 year**.

---

## Why You Should Can Your Own Bone Broth

- **Shelf-Stable Storage:** Preserves freezer space while keeping rich broth ready on your pantry shelf instantly.
- **Superior Nutrient Extraction:** Long 12–24 hour slow simmering breaks down bone cartilage into gelatinous collagen broth.
- **Zero Waste:** Converts kitchen vegetable scraps and leftover roast bones into healthy nutrient-dense broth.

---

## Pressure Canning Safety Essentials

Because bone broth is a **low-acid food (pH above 4.6)**, water-bath canning is **UNSAFE** and can lead to botulism contamination. You MUST use a pressure canner.

1. **Roast Bones First:** Roasting beef marrow bones or chicken carcasses at 200°C (400°F) for 30 minutes adds deep amber color and caramel flavor.
2. **Add Apple Cider Vinegar:** 2 tablespoons of acid breaks down calcium and collagen from bones during simmering.
3. **Leave 1-Inch Headspace:** Leave exactly 1 inch of air space at the top of mason jars before processing to allow expansion under pressure.
4. **Process Ratios:** Process pint jars for 20 minutes (or quart jars for 25 minutes) at 10 lbs pressure (adjusted for altitude).

---

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I preserve bone broth in a water-bath canner?
**No, absolute safety rule:** Low-acid foods like broth, meats, and un-pickled vegetables require pressure canning temperatures (240°F / 116°C) to destroy botulism spores.

### How do I know if the jar sealed properly?
After cooling for 24 hours, press the center of the lid. If it stays flat and doesn't pop up and down, the vacuum seal is intact.
