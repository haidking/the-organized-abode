Dutch Bros Coffee has a devoted following that extends well beyond the Pacific Northwest where it originated. Among the most consistently ordered drinks on their menu is the Golden Eagle — a blended espresso drink built around caramel, vanilla, and a creamy half-and-half base that Dutch Bros calls "Kick Me Mix."

The Golden Eagle is not a complicated drink conceptually, but making it at home requires understanding what each component is actually doing — because substituting the wrong thing in the wrong place produces something that tastes close but not quite right.

---

## What the Golden Eagle Actually Contains

The Dutch Bros Golden Eagle Blended has four core components:

1. **Espresso** — The foundation. Dutch Bros uses a medium-dark espresso blend. At home, 2 shots of strong espresso or ½ cup of cold brew concentrate gives you the right coffee intensity.

2. **Vanilla Syrup** — Dutch Bros uses their proprietary vanilla syrup, which is slightly sweeter than standard coffee shop syrups. Torani or Monin vanilla syrup are the closest commercially available equivalents. Avoid imitation vanilla extract — it reads as artificial at cold temperatures.

3. **Caramel Sauce** — Dutch Bros uses a thick caramel drizzle both blended in and on top. Ghirardelli caramel sauce is thicker and richer than the squeeze-bottle caramel syrup most grocery stores sell, and it is the closest match to the Dutch Bros version. Using a thin caramel syrup will make the drink sweeter but less creamy and caramel-flavored.

4. **Half-and-Half (Kick Me Mix)** — This is the defining characteristic of the Golden Eagle. Most blended coffee drinks use whole milk or heavy cream. Dutch Bros uses a half-and-half base for their Kick Me Mix, which creates a richer, creamier texture than milk but is not as heavy as a cream-dominant drink. This is what gives the Golden Eagle its particular smooth, drinkable richness.

---

## The Blending Technique Matters

The texture of a blended coffee drink is determined almost entirely by the ice-to-liquid ratio and blending time. Common mistakes:

**Too much ice, not enough liquid:** The drink is thick and hard to sip, with an icy rather than creamy texture. Ice should be roughly equal in volume to the liquid base.

**Too little ice:** The drink is thin and watery, melts within minutes.

**Under-blending:** Visible ice chunks, uneven consistency. Blend for at least 45–60 seconds on high to achieve smooth texture.

**Over-blending:** The friction of long blending warms the drink and melts excess ice, making it thin and watery. Blend until smooth, then stop.

The correct ratio for a single serving is approximately: **¾ cup ice to ½ cup liquid** (espresso + half-and-half combined).

## Making It Without an Espresso Machine

If you do not have an espresso machine, **cold brew concentrate** is the most effective substitute. Standard cold brew concentrate (sold pre-made in bottles, or made at home at a 1:4 coffee-to-water ratio) has a similar strength and dark roast character to espresso without the hot temperature, which is actually better for a blended drink — hot espresso melts ice instantly before blending.

If using a Moka pot, brew at the strongest setting and let it cool to room temperature or chill briefly before blending.

Instant espresso powder dissolved in a small amount of cold water (1 teaspoon per shot) is the weakest substitute but still functional for a blended drink where espresso flavor competes with caramel and vanilla.

## Customization Options

**Lighter:** Replace the half-and-half with whole milk and reduce the caramel sauce by half. The drink is thinner but significantly lower in calories.

**Richer:** Add 2 tablespoons of heavy cream to the half-and-half base. Closer to a Dutch Bros "Annihilator" profile.

**Less sweet:** Cut the vanilla syrup from 2 tablespoons to 1 tablespoon and use a sugar-free caramel sauce. The espresso flavor comes through more strongly.

**Extra caramel:** Drizzle an extra tablespoon of caramel sauce around the inside of the cup before pouring the blended drink, then add another drizzle on top. This creates the visible caramel swirl look and adds caramel flavor at multiple points in each sip.
