In professional French kitchens, browned butter is known as *beurre noisette* — literally "hazelnut butter." It is one of the simplest culinary techniques in existence, yet it delivers an unmatched nutty, toasted, deeply complex flavor profile to both sweet and savory dishes.

---

## The Science of Browning Butter

Butter consists of roughly **80% butterfat, 16-18% water, and 1-2% milk solids (proteins and sugars)**.

When you heat butter in a pan:
1. Water melts and evaporates (causing the butter to bubble and crackle).
2. Once the water is gone, the temperature rises.
3. The milk solids sink to the bottom of the pan and undergo the **Maillard reaction** — the chemical reaction between amino acids and reducing sugars that creates deep golden color and nutty flavor compounds.

---

## Visual Stages of Brown Butter

- **Stage 1: Melting (1-2 mins)** — Butter melts completely and begins to foam.
- **Stage 2: Foaming & Crackling (2-3 mins)** — Water boils off; white foam covers the surface.
- **Stage 3: Browning & Nutty Aroma (4-5 mins)** — Foam settles, golden flecks appear at the bottom, and a distinct hazelnut aroma fills the kitchen.
- **Critical Action:** Immediately pour into a cool heat-proof bowl to prevent burning!
