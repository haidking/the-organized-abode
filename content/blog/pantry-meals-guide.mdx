When the fridge looks bare at the end of the week, food delivery feels like the easiest option. But if you keep just **5 fundamental pantry staples** in stock — eggs, rice, olive oil, garlic, and canned tomatoes — you have the building blocks for over 30 genuinely different meals.

The key insight is this: most of cooking is about technique applied to the same ingredients, not about having 40 different groceries. Professional cooks understand that a handful of high-quality staples, combined in different ways at different temperatures, produce radically different results.

---

## The 5 Core Building Blocks

**1. Large Fresh Eggs**
Eggs are the most versatile protein in any kitchen. They work as a binder, a protein source, a sauce enricher, and a complete meal on their own. They scramble, fry, poach, and bake. When added to a finished tomato sauce, a cracked egg cooks gently and absorbs the surrounding flavors.

**2. Jasmine or Long-Grain White Rice**
Rice is the foundation of dozens of cuisines. It can be boiled, fried (best done with leftover cold rice), steamed, or used as the base for grain bowls. A 5 lb bag costs under $6 and feeds a household for weeks. Cooking a large pot on Sunday and refrigerating it overnight means fried rice is always 5 minutes away.

**3. Extra Virgin Olive Oil**
Olive oil is not just a cooking fat — it is a flavoring agent. Used at the beginning of cooking, it carries the flavor of garlic and aromatics throughout the dish. Used as a finishing drizzle, it adds richness and fruitiness. The difference between good and great home cooking often comes down to using oil with actual flavor rather than neutral vegetable oil.

**4. Fresh Garlic Cloves**
Garlic is one of the most powerful aromatic ingredients in cooking. Sliced thin, it becomes sweet and mellow when cooked slowly in olive oil. Minced and cooked briefly at high heat, it adds sharpness and punch. Roasted whole, it caramelizes into a sweet, spreadable paste. A single head of garlic contains 10–12 cloves and costs under $1.

**5. Canned Crushed or Whole Tomatoes**
San Marzano-style canned tomatoes are arguably the single best value purchase in any grocery store. A $2 can contains tomatoes that were picked and preserved at peak ripeness — often better than off-season fresh tomatoes. Crushed tomatoes form the base of pasta sauces, shakshuka, soups, and braises. Whole canned tomatoes can be crushed by hand to control the texture.

## Breakfasts You Can Make

- **Classic Fried Egg on Rice:** Fry an egg in olive oil until the white is set but the yolk is still runny. Serve over warm jasmine rice with a pinch of flaky salt and a splash of soy sauce if available.
- **Shakshuka (Eggs in Tomato Sauce):** Sauté sliced garlic in olive oil, add crushed tomatoes and a pinch of cumin, simmer for 10 minutes, then crack eggs directly into the sauce and cover until whites are set.
- **Garlic Scrambled Eggs:** Heat olive oil with one minced garlic clove for 30 seconds over medium heat, then add beaten eggs. Scramble slowly over low heat for creamier results.
- **Tomato Rice Porridge:** Simmer leftover rice in a small amount of crushed tomatoes thinned with water. Season with salt and top with a fried egg.
- **Crispy Garlic Fried Rice:** Toss cold leftover rice in a hot oiled pan with sliced garlic until the grains are separate and slightly crisped. Push to the side, scramble an egg in the same pan, then combine.

## Lunches and Light Meals

- **10-Minute Tomato Pasta Sauce:** Sauté 4 smashed garlic cloves in olive oil over medium heat until golden. Add a can of crushed tomatoes, a pinch of salt, and simmer for 10 minutes. Use on pasta, rice, or as a dipping sauce.
- **Garlic Tomato Rice Bowl:** Sauté garlic in olive oil, add a scoop of cooked rice and a few tablespoons of crushed tomatoes, toss to coat and heat through. Top with a fried or poached egg.
- **Spanish-Style Rice:** Toast raw rice in olive oil with garlic for 2 minutes before adding water. The toasted starch creates a nutty, separate-grain result very different from plain boiled rice.

---

## Dinners

- **Shakshuka for Two:** The shakshuka described above, scaled up with more tomatoes, can serve 2 adults as a satisfying dinner alongside bread or plain rice.
- **Braised Garlic Tomato Eggs:** Slow-simmer a can of tomatoes with 8–10 whole garlic cloves in olive oil for 25 minutes until the garlic is tender and the sauce is deeply reduced. Add eggs and finish gently. A deeply flavorful dish with almost no effort.
- **Tomato Rice Casserole:** Combine cooked rice, crushed tomatoes, olive oil, and garlic in an oven-safe dish. Bake at 375°F for 20 minutes until the top is slightly caramelized.

## The Combination Logic

The reason 5 ingredients can produce 30+ distinct meals is because each technique creates a fundamentally different product:

- **Garlic sliced vs. minced vs. smashed** = three different flavor profiles in the same dish
- **Tomatoes raw vs. briefly sautéed vs. long-simmered** = three different flavor intensities
- **Rice freshly cooked vs. leftover cold** = two completely different textures
- **Eggs fried vs. scrambled vs. poached vs. baked into sauce** = four different eating experiences

The math compounds quickly.
