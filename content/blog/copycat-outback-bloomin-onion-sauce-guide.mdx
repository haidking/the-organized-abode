Outback Steakhouse's Bloomin' Onion dipping sauce is one of those condiments that is good enough to eat with a spoon. The Bloomin' Onion itself is excellent, but the sauce is what people remember — creamy, slightly spicy, vaguely smoky, with an underlying tang that makes it more complex than ranch or plain aioli.

The formula is not complicated, but the ratios matter more than most copycat recipes acknowledge, and there is one step — a 30-minute minimum rest in the refrigerator — that separates a sauce that tastes close from one that tastes right.

---

## What Makes This Sauce Distinctive

The Outback sauce belongs to the category of horseradish-mayo sauces, related to but distinct from classic remoulade and cocktail sauce. It has four defining characteristics:

**Creaminess from mayonnaise:** Full-fat mayo provides the body and mouthfeel. Low-fat mayonnaise makes the sauce thinner and less satisfying.

**Heat from prepared horseradish:** Prepared horseradish (grated horseradish root preserved in vinegar) — not creamed horseradish sauce, which contains additional dairy and has a milder flavor. Prepared horseradish delivers a sinus-clearing, sharp heat that is qualitatively different from hot sauce heat. It activates in the back of the nasal passage rather than on the tongue.

**Depth from smoked paprika:** This is the smoky, slightly sweet background note that makes the sauce taste like it has been prepared by someone who knows what they're doing. Regular paprika produces a pale, flat version. Smoked paprika (pimentón) is essential.

**Brightness from ketchup and vinegar:** Ketchup adds a small amount of sweetness and acid. A small addition of apple cider vinegar (optional but recommended) adds additional sharpness that tightens the flavor.

---

## The Correct Ratios

This sauce does not have public documentation of the exact Outback recipe, but testing against the original has established a formula that is consistently close:

- ½ cup mayonnaise
- 2 tablespoons prepared horseradish (not creamed)
- 2 tablespoons ketchup
- 1 teaspoon smoked paprika
- ½ teaspoon garlic powder
- ½ teaspoon onion powder
- ¼ teaspoon cayenne pepper
- ¼ teaspoon dried oregano
- Salt and black pepper to taste
- Optional: ½ teaspoon apple cider vinegar

Whisk all ingredients together until completely smooth. Do not skip the apple cider vinegar if you have it — it adds a brightness that the ketchup alone does not provide.

---

## The Rest Period: Why It Matters

This is the step that most copycat recipes skip, and it produces a noticeably inferior result: **refrigerate the sauce for a minimum of 30 minutes before serving.** Longer is better — 2 hours produces a noticeably more developed sauce than 30 minutes.

The reason is that dried herbs and garlic powder need time to fully hydrate in the fatty mayo base and release their flavor compounds. Dried oregano and garlic powder stirred into mayonnaise and tasted immediately has a raw, powder-y edge. After 30–60 minutes in the refrigerator, those same dried ingredients have rehydrated and their flavor has dispersed evenly throughout the sauce — the result tastes rounder, more integrated, and more like the restaurant version.

The horseradish also "blooms" in the refrigerator as it continues to release allyl isothiocyanate (the compound responsible for the heat) into the surrounding fat. Fresh-mixed sauce is spicier; rested sauce has a more even, developed heat.

## Adjusting the Heat Level

**Milder:** Reduce cayenne to ⅛ teaspoon and horseradish to 1 tablespoon. The resulting sauce is still tangy and flavorful but accessible for heat-sensitive guests.

**Spicier:** Increase horseradish to 3 tablespoons and cayenne to ½ teaspoon. Add ½ teaspoon of hot sauce (Frank's RedHot or Tabasco).

**Extra smoky:** Double the smoked paprika to 2 teaspoons and add ½ teaspoon of liquid smoke. This produces a significantly bolder, barbecue-adjacent version.

## 12 Uses Beyond Onion Rings

The sauce's specific flavor profile — creamy, tangy, smoky, mildly spicy — works well in far more contexts than a dipping sauce for fried food.

1. **Burger spread** — replace ketchup and mustard on smash burgers
2. **French fry dip** — works especially well with seasoned wedge fries
3. **Chicken tender dipping sauce** — pairs better than plain ranch with anything fried
4. **Steak accompaniment** — serve alongside grilled or pan-seared steak as a condiment
5. **Roast beef sandwich spread** — classic horseradish-mayo application
6. **Deviled egg filling** — replace plain mayo with the Bloomin' Onion sauce mixture
7. **Potato salad dressing** — replace the plain mayo dressing with this sauce, reduce salt
8. **Coleslaw dressing** — adds smokiness and tang to creamy coleslaw
9. **Fish taco sauce** — excellent with battered or grilled fish
10. **Egg wrap spread** — on a whole wheat tortilla with fried eggs and turkey
11. **Crudité dip** — as a vegetable dipping sauce for raw vegetables
12. **Baked potato topping** — as a condiment alongside or instead of sour cream

## Storage

Refrigerate in a covered container for up to 2 weeks. The flavor actually continues to improve for the first 3–4 days as the dried herbs and horseradish fully develop. Do not freeze — mayonnaise-based sauces separate and become grainy when frozen and thawed.

The horseradish heat typically mellows slightly over a week of storage as the isothiocyanate compounds break down — if you want maximum heat, use within 3 days.
